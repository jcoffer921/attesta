# Significant noncompliance (SNC): copper exceedances and a late July DMR

Short answer: yes, both facts likely put you in SNC, under two different criteria. These are the federal SNC criteria for NPDES permittees in 40 CFR 123.45, Appendix A (the basis for EPA's national SNC tracking). Your state (e.g., PA DEP) may apply them with its own wording. I am answering from memory of the regulation, so verify against the current text and your permit.

## 1. Copper monthly average about 25% over the limit in 2 of 6 months

Criterion: Technical Review Criteria (TRC) violation. It applies to effluent limit violations.

- Copper is a metal and is a Group II pollutant (toxics/metals) under Appendix A. Group II uses a TRC factor of 1.2, meaning a measurement that is 20% or more above the limit is a TRC exceedance. (Group I, e.g. BOD, TSS, oil and grease, ammonia, uses 1.4, or 40%.)
- A reading about 25% over (ratio about 1.25) exceeds the 1.2 factor, so each of those two monthly averages is a TRC exceedance.
- SNC is triggered when 33% or more of all measurements for that pollutant in a six-month period are TRC exceedances. Two of six monthly averages is 33.3%, which meets the threshold.

Caveats:
- The percentage counts all measurements for that pollutant/limit in the six-month window. If you also have a daily maximum, or multiple outfalls, evaluated separately, the denominator changes. Evaluate each limit on its own. For the monthly-average limit alone, 2 of 6 qualifies.
- The "about 25%" must actually be at least 20% over (reported ratio >= 1.2). If either month was really 18% over, it would not count as a TRC exceedance and you would fall below 33%.
- The two months must fall in the same consecutive six-month window (they do, if both are in the last six months).

Chronic criterion (does not apply): any pollutant, any magnitude over the limit, in 4 or more of 6 consecutive months. You have 2 of 6, so this is not met. Still, a third and fourth exceedance in the window would trigger it even at small magnitudes.

Also note: SNC status aside, each exceedance is a violation in its own right, and you should check whether the permit requires 24-hour or 5-day notification (copper exceedances typically do not unless they are a bypass, upset, or endanger health; check Part II/Part A of the permit).

## 2. July DMR filed 45 days late

Criterion: Reporting violation. Appendix A treats failure to provide a required report (DMR, or a compliance schedule/progress report) 30 days or more after the due date as SNC.

- 45 days is more than 30, so the late DMR is itself an SNC-level reporting violation.
- Filing it late stops the missing-report condition, but it does not erase the violation. The violation is recorded for the period it was late.

## Net result

| Issue | Criterion | SNC? |
|---|---|---|
| Copper 2 of 6 months, about 25% over | TRC, Group II (1.2x), 33% of measurements in 6 months | Yes (2/6 = 33%), subject to caveats |
| Copper, chronic (4 of 6 months) | Chronic | No (2 of 6) |
| July DMR 45 days late | Reporting, 30+ days overdue | Yes |

## What to do

- Confirm exact ratios (reported value / limit) for the two months and the full measurement count for the window.
- Expect the regulator to see SNC on the next quarterly noncompliance report and to consider enforcement. SNC is a tracking category, not itself a penalty, but it raises enforcement priority.
- Document the cause and corrective action for copper, and why the DMR was late. Corrective action and timely reporting going forward are what move you off SNC in later review periods.
- Consider proactive contact with the regulator.
