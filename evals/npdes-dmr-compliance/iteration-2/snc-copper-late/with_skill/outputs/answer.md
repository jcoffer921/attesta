# Does either issue put us in significant noncompliance (SNC)?

**Short answer:** Both can. The copper exceedances likely meet the Technical Review Criteria (TRC) for SNC, but not the Chronic criterion. The 45-day-late July DMR is a reporting violation that also counts toward SNC. SNC is assigned by EPA (ICIS-NPDES) or the state, not by you, so treat the following as an indicative assessment, not an official determination.

## 1. Copper monthly average exceeded by ~25% in 2 of 6 months

Criteria for monthly-average limits (40 CFR 123.45 App. A, Category I):

| Criterion | Test | Your facts | Met? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic | Limit exceeded by any amount in 4 or more months of a 6-month period | 2 months | No |
| Technical Review Criteria (TRC) | Monthly average at or above 1.4x the limit (Group I) or 1.2x (Group II), in 2 or more months of a 6-month period | ~1.25x in 2 months | Likely yes, if copper is Group II |

- Copper is a metal. Most metals fall in Group II (the 1.2x threshold). Group I is oxygen demand, solids, nutrients, oil and grease, minerals and some metals. At 1.25x you exceed 1.2x but not 1.4x, so the group matters. If copper were Group I, you would not meet TRC. Confirm copper's group against the App. A list; the skill notes the full Group I/II lists are not completely verified.
- "About 25%" is too loose. The threshold is a ratio of 1.2 or more of the limit, so check the actual values. Roughly 1.25x leaves a small margin, and rounding or the exact averages could matter.
- The 2 months must fall within the same 6-month period, and the limit must be a monthly average (as you describe).
- Non-detects in the averaging can change the computed average. Verify how the permit directs non-detects to be handled before relying on the 1.25x figure.

Conclusion: no Chronic SNC (2 of 6 months, not 4). Probable TRC SNC.

## 2. July DMR filed 45 days late

- Reporting violations, such as DMRs 30 or more days late, count toward SNC. Nationally they account for over half of facilities in SNC.
- 45 days exceeds the 30-day threshold, so this would be a reporting-based SNC trigger. The 30-day figure itself is general domain knowledge (not fully verified against a primary source), so confirm it with the state or EPA, or against the permit and program guidance.
- The late filing is separate from the copper exceedances and is a violation on its own, independent of the effluent numbers.

## What this means in practice

- You could be flagged for SNC on two independent grounds: effluent (TRC, if copper is Group II) and reporting (late DMR).
- Report all noncompliance with the DMR (122.41(l)(7)). Separately, check whether any exceedance triggered the 24-hour oral and 5-day written notice requirements (those apply to violations of listed maximum daily limits, bypasses, upsets and health/environment endangerment). Monthly-average exceedances alone are generally reported with the DMR.
- Keep records of samples, calibration and reports for at least 3 years.
- Check the actual permit and the state program (for example, Pennsylvania DEP) for any stricter or different requirements. The permit is the authority.

## Caveats

- SNC is determined by EPA or the state, not self-declared.
- Attesta can only preview these criteria as informational flags (such as "would meet TRC"), clearly labeled indicative. It does not model late-DMR violations yet.
