**Short answer: not a violation as reported, but the result is not usable as-is, and you may need a rerun.**

**Why:** "<5 ug/L" means copper was not detected at a quantitation/reporting level of 5 ug/L. That is above your 3 ug/L daily max, so the result cannot show whether you met the limit. A non-detect at a level higher than the permit limit is inconclusive, not an exceedance (and not proof of compliance).

**What to do:**
1. Ask the lab about the method and its QL/MDL. PA DEP expects analytical methods sensitive enough to measure at or below the permit limit (or the Quantitation Limit listed in the permit, often in Part A/Part C). Check the permit for a stated QL for copper. If the lab can reach 3 ug/L or lower (e.g., EPA 200.8 ICP-MS), have the sample re-analyzed if it is still within holding time, or use a lower-QL method on the next sample.
2. If the permit lists a QL and the lab met it, the "<QL" result is reported as such and counts as in compliance.

**On the DMR (eDMR):**
- Enter the value as "<5" (use the less-than qualifier and the actual detection/reporting level, 5 ug/L) in the Daily Max field. Do not enter 0, 2.5, or 5 as a number.
- Do not use a NODI code for this; NODI codes are for no discharge, no sample taken, etc. Use the "<" qualifier and report the lab's level.
- For the monthly average, follow the permit and PA DEP DMR instructions for non-detects (generally treat as zero if all values are non-detect, and report "<" at the QL). Only a detected value goes into averages as measured.
- Add a comment on the DMR noting the QL was above the permit limit, the method used, and that no exceedance is indicated.

**Bottom line for your plant manager:** No confirmed violation, but a 5 ug/L reporting level cannot demonstrate compliance with a 3 ug/L limit. Fix the lab method or QL going forward (and rerun the October sample if possible) so it does not become an inadequate-monitoring issue with DEP. Confirm details against your permit's QL language and DEP's DMR instructions.
