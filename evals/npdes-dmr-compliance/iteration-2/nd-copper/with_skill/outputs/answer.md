**Short answer: we can't call it a violation yet, and we can't call it compliant either. It needs a decision before the DMR goes in.**

**Why:** The lab's `<5 ug/L` means copper was below their quantitation limit (QL) of 5. Our daily max is 3. Because the QL (5) is higher than the limit (3), the result cannot show whether we met the limit. It is not an exceedance, because we have no detected value above 3. It is not a pass, because the actual concentration could be anywhere from 0 to 5.

**How to put it on the DMR (PA):**
- Report the result as `<5` (the `<` sign with the QL). Do not enter 0 or "ND", and do not use a No Discharge code.
- If every October result was non-detect, the monthly statistic is also reported as `<5`. If some were detected, the average gets a `<` too, using the QL for each non-detect.
- Zero may replace the QL only if the permit's limit is below the most sensitive detection limit and the parameter is listed in 25 Pa. Code Ch. 16 App. A Tables 2A/2B. Check our permit's Part C for this.

**What to do now:**
1. Check the permit (Part C and the copper requirements) for the required QL or method. If the permit requires a QL at or below 3 ug/L, the lab's 5 is a lab or method problem. Ask the lab whether it can rerun with a lower QL, or whether the sample can be re-analyzed within hold time.
2. If a lower QL can't be achieved, add a comment on the DMR explaining the non-detect and the QL. Do not mark it as an excursion yourself.
3. Escalate to DEP on how they want it handled if the permit is silent. The call on whether `<5` against a limit of 3 is treated as a violation is DEP's and the permit's, not ours.

**Caveats:** This is based on PA DEP DMR guidance, which is partly dated, and the permit text is the authority. I haven't seen your permit, so confirm the QL requirement and the non-detect clause there.

I'm checking the permit's required detection level and asking the lab about a lower reporting limit before we submit."
