**Short answer: not a confirmed violation, and not a pass. It is unresolved until the lab's detection limit is addressed.**

- A result of `<5 ug/L` means copper was below the lab's quantitation limit (QL) of 5. Your daily max is 3. Because the QL (5) is above the limit (3), the result can neither show compliance nor prove an exceedance. Treat it as "needs review", not "violation" and not "pass".
- Do not report it as 0, "ND", or "No Discharge".

**How to put it on the DMR (PA DEP convention):**
- Report the daily maximum as `<5` ug/L (the `<` symbol with the QL). If every result for October was non-detect, report `<QL` as the value. If you had several samples, the `<` goes on the statistic if any sample was non-detect.
- Do not use a NODI code (such as B or GG) when you have a result; a row takes a value or a code, not both.
- Add a comment that the QL (5 ug/L) exceeds the permit limit (3 ug/L).

**Possible exception:** zero may be substituted for the non-detect only if the permit itself sets a limit below the most sensitive detection limit and copper is listed in 25 Pa. Code Ch. 16 App. A Tables 2A/2B. Check Part C of your permit.

**Before you submit:**
1. Ask the lab whether a lower QL is available. A method sensitive enough to measure 3 ug/L or less (and, ideally, the permit's required method) resolves this. Check the permit for a required analytical method and QL.
2. Confirm in the permit how non-detects are to be handled, since the permit is the authority.
3. If the lab cannot get below 3 ug/L, document this and ask DEP how they want it handled before certifying the DMR.
