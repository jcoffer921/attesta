**Short answer: not a violation on its face, but you can't call it compliant yet. The lab's detection level is too high for your limit.**

**Why**
- "<5 ug/L" means copper was not detected at a quantification level (QL) of 5 ug/L. That does not show the discharge exceeded 3 ug/L.
- It also does not prove it was under 3. The lab's QL (5) is above your daily max (3), so the result can't demonstrate compliance.
- Check your permit for a specified QL or required method for copper. DEP permits usually include one. If the permit QL is, for example, 5 or higher, a "<5" result is treated as compliant. If the permit QL is below 5, this lab result is not acceptable data.

**On the DMR**
- Do not enter 0, 3, or 5. Enter the result as "<5" (less-than qualifier with the lab's QL) in the daily max field in eDMR.
- Add a comment explaining that the result is below the lab QL and that the QL is above the permit limit.
- Do not check the box for an exceedance based on this result alone.
- The October DMR is due by the 28th of November.

**What to do now**
1. Ask the lab which method they used and their reporting limit. Total recoverable copper by ICP-MS (EPA 200.8) routinely gets down to about 0.5 ug/L, which is well below 3.
2. Switch to a method with a QL at or below the permit limit for all future samples.
3. If the October sample is still within holding time, have it re-analyzed with the lower-QL method. If not, note that on the DMR.
4. If DEP questions the result, you have the documentation showing a good-faith effort.

*This is general guidance. Confirm the QL and reporting language in your actual permit (Part A/C) or with your DEP regional office.*

**Reply to plant manager (one line):** "Copper came back non-detect at <5 ug/L, but the lab's detection level is above our 3 ug/L limit, so it's not a confirmed violation and not confirmed compliance. We'll report it as '<5' with a comment and have the lab use a lower-detection method going forward."
