# Is the facility in significant noncompliance for BOD?

**Short answer: on the numbers you gave, no. The BOD monthly-average pattern does not meet either effluent SNC criterion. But the facility is still in violation in those three months, and it is close to the chronic threshold.**

One caveat first: SNC is assigned by EPA (via ICIS-NPDES) or the state, not by the permittee. What follows is an indicative screen against the criteria, not an official determination.

## The criteria (40 CFR 123.45 App. A, monthly-average effluent limits)

**1. Chronic criterion: limit exceeded, by any amount, in 4 or more months of a 6-month period.**
- Your facility exceeded in 3 of 6 months.
- 3 < 4, so this is **not met**. One more exceedance month inside the same 6-month window would meet it.

**2. Technical Review Criteria (TRC): monthly average at or above 1.4x the limit (Group I) or 1.2x (Group II), in 2 or more months of a 6-month period.**
- BOD is an oxygen-demand parameter, so it is **Group I**. The threshold is 1.4x the limit.
- A 25% exceedance is 1.25x, which is below 1.4x, so those months do not count toward TRC for BOD.
- The 1.2x Group II threshold does not apply to BOD. 1.25x would have cleared it, but BOD is Group I.
- TRC is **not met**. The key number to check is whether any month actually hit 1.4x (for example, a month "about 25%" over that is really 40% over).

## What this does and does not mean

- **Not SNC is not the same as compliant.** Three months over the monthly-average limit are still permit violations and exceedances. They must be reported on the DMR, and the facility should expect enforcement attention and need a corrective-action plan.
- **The facility is one month from chronic SNC.** In a rolling 6-month window, a fourth exceedance of any size would trigger it. Treat the next month as high risk.
- **Check the 6-month window.** The test is any 6-month period, evaluated on a rolling basis. If your three exceedance months fall inside a window that also includes other exceedances (for example a month where you were "fine" on average but had a daily-maximum problem), re-check.
- **Other things can also make a facility SNC**, which I cannot rule out from what you told me:
  - Reporting violations, such as DMRs 30 or more days late or missing. Nationally these account for over half of facilities in SNC.
  - Exceedances of other parameters or other limit types (TRSS and chronic counts are tracked per parameter and limit).
  - Other categories such as compliance schedule violations or unauthorized bypasses.
- **Daily-maximum violations** may trigger the 24-hour oral and 5-day written notification requirement (40 CFR 122.41(l)(6)), independent of SNC.

## What to verify against the permit and DMRs

- The exact statistical base. Is the limit a monthly average, and does the permit define the average differently (for example, a geometric mean)?
- All samples are included in the average. All monitoring done with approved methods must be included, even if more frequent than required. Dropping samples to improve an average is not allowed.
- Units match the permit. A unit mismatch is a data error, not a pass or fail.
- Whether the "25%" is the exact ratio per month. A month at 1.40x or higher changes the answer (TRC needs two such months in the window).
- Mass-loading limits (lbs/day) and percent-removal requirements for BOD, if the permit has them. These are separate limits with their own tests.

## Bottom line

| Test | Threshold | Facility | Met? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic | exceed in 4+ of 6 months | 3 of 6 | No |
| TRC (Group I, BOD) | 1.4x limit in 2+ of 6 months | about 1.25x | No |

Not in SNC for BOD on these facts, but in violation in 3 months and one exceedance away from chronic SNC.
