**Short answer: No, based on what you've described, this is not significant noncompliance (SNC). But it is a real violation pattern, and it is one more exceedance away from chronic SNC.**

## Criteria (EPA SNC definition, 40 CFR 123.45 Appendix A, as applied by EPA and states such as PA DEP)

Effluent limit SNC is judged per pollutant, per outfall, over a rolling 6-month window, using two tests.

**1. Technical Review Criteria (TRC) - magnitude**
- Group I pollutants (oxygen-demanding substances such as BOD/CBOD, COD, TOC, TSS, oil and grease, nutrients): an exceedance counts toward the TRC if the measured value is 1.4x (40% over) the limit or more.
- Group II pollutants (most metals and toxics): 1.2x (20% over) or more.
- SNC is triggered when 66% or more of the measurements in the 6-month period exceed the TRC multiple.

Your case: BOD is Group I, so the multiple is 40%. A 25% exceedance is below 1.4x, so none of these months count as TRC exceedances. The 66% test is not met either, since only 50% of months exceeded at all.

**2. Chronic criterion - frequency**
- SNC is triggered when the same pollutant exceeds its limit (any magnitude) in 4 or more months out of 6.

Your case: 3 of 6 months. Not met. It would be met if a fourth month in the window exceeds.

## Other SNC categories to check
The above covers only effluent limits. SNC can also arise from:
- Failure to submit a DMR more than 30 days late.
- Missing a compliance schedule milestone by 90+ days, or failing to submit a progress report 30+ days late.
- Any single violation (bypass, upset, spill, etc.) that the regulator determines caused or threatened health or the environment, or any violation of an enforcement order.

## Caveats and practical notes
- Check all limit types. If BOD also has a weekly-average or daily-maximum limit and those were exceeded, count them. Evaluate individual measurements against TRC, not only the monthly averages.
- Be sure the 25% is relative to the limit (value / limit = 1.25), and that the same units/basis (concentration vs. mass loading) is used.
- Check other parameters (TSS, ammonia, etc.) separately; each is evaluated on its own.
- The window is rolling, so re-check each month. The three exceedances sit within the window only until they age out.
- Each exceedance is still a permit violation. It must be reported on the DMR, and any required notifications, corrective action, and possible enforcement (e.g., a consent order or penalties) can apply even without SNC. Non-SNC violations are still tracked as "Category 1/2" noncompliance in EPA's reporting.
- SNC is formally a regulator/EPA determination (reported in the QNCR), so confirm with your state agency, as states may apply stricter local criteria.

Bottom line: 3 exceedances of 25% on a Group I pollutant fail both SNC tests (not 40%+ magnitude, not 4 of 6 months), so no SNC, but you are one more exceedance from chronic SNC and should act now.
